# Lens Deck

Use the six lenses as starting questions for a direct run with a grounded brief. When the premise leaves the central decision open, first follow [the reading procedure](reframing.md). Within each reading, choose any lens that could change the decision, or generate a card directly from the reading. The six questions below are available throughout.

## 1. Body / embodiment

**Question:** What does the object do physically, and what does it refuse to do?

Seeds:

- remove the screen;
- make it silent;
- give it a body that cannot move;
- make it wait before acting;
- make it touch or not touch;
- make it share the user's space without occupying it.

Good cards change the user's physical behaviour, not just the interface.

## 2. Ritual / moment

**Question:** When does it intervene, and what moment does it replace?

Seeds:

- before the question;
- during the answer;
- after the decision;
- at the end of the day;
- at the start of a meeting;
- when the user is about to give up.

Avoid "always on." A ritual has a beginning and an end.

## 3. Relationship / role

**Question:** Who is it to the user: coach, witness, adversary, mirror, gatekeeper, or companion?

Seeds:

- it never answers;
- it answers only with a question;
- it argues the opposite;
- it remembers what the user decided;
- it represents a future self;
- it represents someone affected by the decision.

A role change is stronger than a feature change.

## 4. Material / structure

**Question:** What form makes the behaviour inevitable?

Seeds:

- no screen, only light;
- no app, only a physical object;
- one button;
- a disposable card;
- a shared object between two people;
- a device that cannot be personalized.

Do not design CMF here. Design the affordance.

## 5. Service / system

**Question:** Who owns the data, pays, maintains, and ends the service?

Seeds:

- subscription vs one-time;
- personal vs shared;
- local vs cloud;
- school/studio vs home;
- anonymous vs accountable;
- repairable vs sealed.

A service card must change a decision, not just a business model.

## 6. Reverse / extreme

**Question:** What if we remove the constraint everyone assumes is necessary?

Seeds:

- no electricity;
- no screen;
- no AI;
- no personal data;
- designed for one extreme user;
- designed to be boring.

Reverse cards are allowed to be strange, but not vague. They still need a substitute and a kill condition.

## Batch recipe

For each lens in a direct run:

1. Write the lens question at the top of the batch.
2. Generate 3–4 deep cards using different seeds; keep up to 6 raw seeds separately.
3. For each card, write the claim before the description.
4. Run the seven checks immediately; do not wait for all lenses.
5. Append to `02_CANDIDATES.md` and `cards/`.
6. Dedupe against earlier lenses before starting the next one.

## Coverage check

For a standard direct run, check coverage and distinctness after exploring the lenses:

- at least 18 deep cards;
- no lens below 3 deep cards;
- at most 2 cards share the same mechanism;
- at least 6 cards are "strange but testable";
- at least 3 cards are uncomfortable for the brief's author.

For a reading-first run, use one batch per reading. Organise cards by the decision each reading opens. Revisit a lens only if it supplies a genuinely different alternative. Allocate the requested or default total across branches according to opportunity. Report uneven coverage and readings still awaiting evidence. Check for duplicated mechanisms across readings.

Raw seeds can be more numerous; they stay in `seeds/` and do not count as candidates.

If every candidate relies on the same untested premise, revisit the reading or the lens question that produced it.
