# Exploring readings of an open premise

Use this procedure when accepting the premise literally would settle the central user, job or purpose by assumption. A reading is a problem worth investigating. A lens is one way to generate interventions once a problem has been identified.

## Before naming an intervention

1. Copy the prompt and separate explicit commitments from intriguing details. A user-specified feature or audience remains a constraint. For a detail the user left open, ask what tension becomes visible if the detail stays, moves to a different setting, or disappears.
2. Draft competing questions whose answers would produce different real decisions. A literal reading can be productive. Other readings can follow a conflict between needs, a relationship or a tradeoff into an accessible setting. These moves are options. Use whichever expose a difference in who acts, what is at stake or which evidence could change the direction.
3. Check the proposed readings against each other. If the same actor makes the same decision against the same alternative, merge the readings or explain the remaining difference. A striking name alone does not establish a separate branch.

## Keep a record in the brief

For each retained reading, write a stable ID and the following fields in the readings section of `00_BRIEF.md`. Put verified findings and sources in `01_SCAN.md` under the same ID.

| Field | What the reader should learn |
|---|---|
| `name`, `q` | A short display name and the actual question the branch will pursue. |
| `premise` | Which commitment was kept, relaxed or moved. Name the tension that survives the move and why the original wording brought it into view. |
| `audience` | Who could experience the problem, in which reachable situation, and which consequential decision they face. Distinguish a future beneficiary from a present-day person who can only test part of the claim. |
| `check` | The least costly credible observation, record or conversation about an actual choice that could support or undermine the problem's importance. Identify what result would change the assessment. |

Use the user's requested breadth or, for a full atlas, aim for roughly 18 distinct deep cards across the surviving readings. Keep promising but unverified questions visible with their next check. Develop richer branches more deeply. A reading may have no viable card yet. Decide how many branches to retain from their substantive differences.

## From reading to design

Generate candidates independently within each reading. Keep the reading ID on the card. Explain the connection in one or two sentences in `insight`. State what tension the prompt exposed, who faces an analogous decision, and how the intervention could change it. This is especially important when the final idea drops the original object or setting. A card's `lens` is optional here.

Look for the cheapest current substitute and an observable consequence of using it. Consider ordinary choices, time already spent, a workaround, displaced resources, exclusion, or an abandoned activity. Draw on available evidence and label inferences and proposed tests. A usability or mechanism test with a convenient proxy can answer a technical question while leaving the actual need entirely open.

Recommend directions whose insight, consequences and next evidence step merit attention. When evidence of need is missing, explain whether the next useful action is a small problem check or whether the reading should remain an open question. The most buildable prototype may be a poor use of the next day. A promising reading may lead to an information service, an experience, a reframed design brief or another form beyond the nouns in the initial prompt.
